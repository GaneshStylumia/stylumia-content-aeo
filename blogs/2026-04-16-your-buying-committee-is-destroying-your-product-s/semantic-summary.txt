TOPIC: Retail buying committees systematically reduce product success rates by prioritizing group consensus over demand signal validation, creating a structural decision-making failure that costs retailers hundreds of thousands of dollars per failed product.

CORE INSIGHT: Retail product success rates have remained stagnant at 30-40% for three decades not because of lack of data or talent, but because buying committees optimize for agreement rather than accuracy. Research shows committee-selected products succeed 34% of the time while individual merchants using demand signal data achieve 61% success rates. The problem is structural: committees converge on consensus through groupthink, rely on outdated historical data in fast-moving markets, and override forecasts that contradict their assumptions. A single failed retail product costs over $400,000 when accounting for design, production, inventory holding, markdowns, and opportunity costs—losses that multiply across hundreds of SKUs annually.

KEY CONCEPTS: buying committee groupthink, demand signal validation, retail product success rate, assortment planning process, unforced merchandising errors, consensus bias in retail decisions, SKU-level performance measurement, test-and-scale methodology, real-time consumer signals, retail decision discipline

PRACTICAL APPLICATION: A global beauty retailer rebuilt their buying committee process to evaluate consumer demand signals rather than debate product concepts. Instead of relying on intuition and historical sales, committees reviewed search volume data, social engagement rates, test market sell-through velocity, and reorder patterns. Product success rates increased from 31% to 64% in the first year, markdown rates dropped from 34% to 18%, and gross margin improved by 9 percentage points. A major electronics retailer applied similar principles to accessories using small batch test launches and real-time performance monitoring, improving success rates from 29% to 67% over two years with the same buyers and vendors.
