TOPIC: Asymmetric inventory allocation is a retail capital management strategy that concentrates inventory investment in high-performing products while minimizing funding for unproven items, treating SKU portfolios like venture capital investments rather than spreading capital equally across all products.

CORE INSIGHT: Most retailers lose money by funding all products equally, despite demand following power laws where 20 percent of SKUs generate 80 percent of profit. Asymmetric inventory allocation solves this by making smaller initial purchases across all products, using early sales data as market tests, then aggressively replenishing proven winners while quickly discontinuing losers. This approach reduces markdowns from 28 percent to 16 percent, improves inventory turns from 5.2 to 6.8 times annually, and increases gross margins by 4 to 6 percentage points without requiring additional capital investment.

KEY CONCEPTS: power law demand distribution, inventory portfolio management, capital concentration, GMROII by SKU quartile, kill criteria implementation, continuous SKU rationalization, demand-driven replenishment, markdown reduction through upstream discipline, inventory turn optimization, open-to-buy reallocation

PRACTICAL APPLICATION: A fashion retailer reduced initial buy depth by 35 percent across all new styles and redirected that capital into in-season replenishment of products showing strong sell-through in the first four weeks. Within 18 months, markdown rates dropped from 28 percent to 16 percent, portfolio inventory turns increased from 5.2 to 6.8, and gross margin improved by 4 points. A grocery chain implemented daily replenishment algorithms prioritizing accelerating SKUs while deprioritizing declining products, improving inventory productivity by 19 percent without increasing total inventory investment. A home goods retailer applied quarterly SKU reviews with automatic discontinuation for bottom 15 percent performers, reducing SKU count by 30 percent while maintaining flat revenue because eliminated products contributed minimal sales.
