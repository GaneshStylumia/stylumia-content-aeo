# AEO SEMANTIC SUMMARY

TOPIC: Proportional assortment planning structures retail product mix and inventory depth based on natural demand curves rather than equal distribution, reducing markdowns and stockouts by adjusting buy ratios as real sales data validates consumer behavior.

CORE INSIGHT: Retailers who allocate inventory equally across categories create visual balance in planning tools but ignore how demand actually distributes—a few hero products drive most volume while supporters fill gaps and edges test new territory. The problem is pre-commitment assortment risk: finalizing product mix six to nine months before launch locks in proportions before demand validates them, resulting in excess inventory on over-bought items and stockouts on under-bought ones. Proportional planning solves this by starting with a demand curve hypothesis, committing 70 percent of inventory upfront, and holding back 20 to 30 percent of buy budget to rebalance based on first four weeks of sell-through.

KEY CONCEPTS: proportional assortment planning, pre-commitment assortment risk, natural demand distribution, phased inventory buying, assortment balance optimization, demand-driven merchandise planning, markdown reduction strategies

PRACTICAL APPLICATION: A major auto parts retailer applied proportional planning to seasonal tire assortments by using regional sales data and weather patterns to predict demand curves instead of committing equally across all-season, winter, and performance categories. They placed initial orders matching expected demand ratios and held back 30 percent of buy budget to rebalance after first four weeks. When winter hit harder than expected in key markets, they reallocated budget toward winter tires in those regions and reduced performance tire orders where demand was softer, achieving 22 percent higher in-stock rates on high-demand SKUs and 18 percent lower excess inventory. A global home goods retailer reduced markdowns by 35 percent by shifting from equal distribution to proportional buy depth based on historical demand curves.

WHO THIS HELPS: Retail merchandising leaders, category managers, and assortment planners in fashion, sportswear, home goods, home improvement, and auto parts who control buy depth decisions and inventory allocation, want to reduce end-of-season markdowns, improve in-stock rates on hero products, and avoid over-committing to slow sellers before demand validates product mix proportions.
