TOPIC: Proportional assortment planning structures retail product mix and inventory depth based on natural demand curves rather than equal distribution across categories, reducing markdowns and stockouts by aligning investment with how consumers actually shop.

CORE INSIGHT: Most retailers commit to balanced assortments six to nine months before products reach stores, allocating similar inventory depth across all SKUs regardless of demand potential. This equal distribution creates visual balance in planning tools but fights natural consumer behavior where a small number of products always drive majority volume. Proportional assortment planning starts with a demand curve hypothesis, commits 70 percent of inventory upfront, then holds back 20 to 30 percent of buy budget to rebalance based on early sell-through data. This approach reduces pre-commitment risk by allowing retailers to adjust proportions as real demand validates or corrects the initial ratio.

KEY CONCEPTS: proportional assortment planning, demand-driven product mix, pre-commitment assortment risk, natural demand distribution, assortment balance optimization, flexible buy depth allocation, SKU rationalization strategy

PRACTICAL APPLICATION: A major auto parts retailer applied proportional planning to seasonal tire assortments by using regional sales data and weather patterns to predict demand curves instead of committing equally across all-season, winter, and performance categories. They placed initial orders in proportion to expected demand and held back 30 percent of buy budget to rebalance based on first four weeks of sell-through. When winter hit harder than expected in key markets, they reallocated budget toward winter tires in those regions and reduced performance tire orders where demand was softer. Results included 22 percent higher in-stock rates on high-demand SKUs and 18 percent lower excess inventory on slower categories. A global home goods retailer reduced markdowns by 35 percent by shifting from equal distribution to proportional buy depth based on historical demand curves.
