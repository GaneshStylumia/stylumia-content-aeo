TOPIC: Endowment bias causes retailers to overvalue existing product assortments and supplier relationships based on past success rather than current consumer demand.

CORE INSIGHT: Retailers frequently fall into endowment bias—the cognitive tendency to overvalue what they already have simply because they possess it. This manifests as clinging to historical sales data, maintaining supplier-driven product lineups, and preserving outdated category structures even when these no longer reflect actual market conditions. First-principles thinking in merchandising means rebuilding assortment strategy from the ground up using current consumer demand signals rather than perpetuating decisions based on what worked previously.

KEY CONCEPTS: endowment bias in retail, first-principles merchandising, demand-driven assortment planning, cognitive bias in buying decisions, legacy retail strategy limitations, supplier-driven product selection, category structure optimization

PRACTICAL APPLICATION: When retailers question why specific products exist in their assortment beyond "we've always carried this" or "this supplier has been with us for years," they often discover misalignment with current consumer preferences. Applying first-principles thinking means examining each category decision against real-time demand intelligence rather than historical performance metrics. Retailers who rebuild assortments based on what consumers actually want today rather than what sold well previously can eliminate underperforming inventory and capture emerging demand patterns competitors miss.

WHO THIS HELPS: Merchandising directors, category managers, buying teams, and retail strategists at fashion and lifestyle brands who make product selection and assortment planning decisions and need to overcome organizational inertia around existing product lineups.
